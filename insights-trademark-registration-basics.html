When to register a trademark, and why timing matters

M.A Salawu & Co·2025-08-05

A question we hear often is whether a business needs to register a trademark before it has fully launched, or whether it is safe to wait until the brand has proven itself. In Nigeria, waiting carries more risk than most business owners expect.

Why first-to-file matters

Nigeria operates a first-to-file trademark system, meaning rights generally belong to whoever files first, not necessarily whoever used the mark first in commerce. In practice, this means a business that has been trading under a name for years, but never registered it, can find that someone else has filed for the same or a similar mark — and now holds the stronger legal position.

What the registration process involves

The process starts with a search to confirm the mark is available and not confusingly similar to an existing registration, followed by filing the application with the Trademarks Registry, publication for opposition, and, assuming no objection is raised, registration itself. The full process typically takes well over a year, which is itself a reason to start earlier rather than later.

What we recommend

For a business preparing to launch a new name, logo, or product line, we recommend clearing and filing the trademark before the brand is publicly used, not after. For an established business that has been trading under an unregistered mark, the priority is to file as soon as possible and to be aware that a search may reveal a conflicting registration that needs to be addressed.

Registration also matters beyond disputes — a registered trademark is an asset that can be licensed, used as security, or valued as part of the business, none of which is straightforward with an unregistered mark.
